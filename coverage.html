What has and hasn't been reported

A check of the coverage against the record, as of Oct 2, 2026, 1:30 pm Eastern.

The Cornell Daily Sun is audited in full because it is the paper most of campus reads on this case. Every item the Sun has published on Chi Phi since Nov 8, 2024 was read: 47 pieces, 33 of them news and 14 opinion (editorials, columns, guest pieces and letters), 31 of them since the lawsuit was reported on Sept 18. For each fact below, the table gives the outlet that first reported it and whether the Sun has reported it. "Not found" means the fact was not in any Sun item when checked; it does not mean the Sun will not report it. National outlets are not audited item by item; what each one first reported is in the table, and a few patterns across all outlets are noted at the end. Corrections to this audit, with a link, go to cornellnightymoon@gmail.com.

What the Sun reported, often first

  • The chapter's suspension, the day of the Nov 8, 2024 crime alert, and President Kotlikoff's Nov 26, 2024 statement, the day it was issued. The vandalism of the house and the IFC's weekend shutdown in Nov 2024. Follow-ups through 2025 on the task force, the survey, and burglaries at the closed house.
  • The lawsuit itself, on Sept 18, 2026, within hours of the earliest report found (CNY Central, 7:21 pm; the Sun, 11:03 pm). The Daily Caller credited the Sun as first. The names of the seven and, on Sept 21, their class years and directory status.
  • Moonies' denial that it served her, in a statement to the Sun (Sept 22), six days before it reached national outlets.
  • Cornell's Sept 21 statement; Cornell's Sept 28 denial that essays were a "sole consequence" and its confirmation of expulsions and suspensions, the same day.
  • The Ithaca Police statement and the mayor's criticism, the evening they were issued (Sept 28).
  • That Cornell's FAQ contradicted the DA on the Snapchat screenshot (Sept 30, 3:27 am), the first outlet found to frame the contradiction.
  • Gov. Hochul's call for a review, Cornell's agreement, the Attorney General's review, Sen. Schumer's and Sen. Gillibrand's statements, the Student Assembly hearing announcement, and Rep. Pelosi's cancellation.
  • Cornell's quiet edits to its FAQ, removing the claim that CUPD shared "all investigatory information" with the DA (Oct 2, 2:44 am; WSKG had reported the first deletion on Oct 1). The special prosecutor's appointment (Oct 2, 11:28 am). The IFC's cancellation of weekend events and the Oct 5 protest, both from internal communications and organizers.

Statements in the Sun that the record contradicts

Each item says whether it ran as news or as opinion. Opinion pieces are not held to a reporting standard, but they are the pieces most readers remember, and each of these ran under the Sun's name without a note once the record changed.

  • Editorial, Sept 21 (opinion). Opens with the seven names as "the seven men named as defendants in a civil complaint filed on Sept. 14 alleging they drugged and gang raped a Cornell student," and closes: "To [the seven names]: We know who you are and we refuse to let you benefit from this University's concealment." The complaint's allegations differ by defendant: it alleges intercourse by four, a forced kiss by one, and pouring ketamine on her body by the remaining two, who it does not allege touched her sexually. Those two say Cornell found them not responsible; President Kotlikoff's breakdown has two found not responsible. CBS New York's reading of the complaint: "Four face rape accusations." Editorial.
  • Editorial, Sept 21 (opinion). "Why was she not informed of the outcome of her own Title IX investigation?" The question treats it as established that she was not. No source found says so. Her attorney described the per-defendant outcomes to CBS New York on Sept 24 (two expelled; others "suspensions, workshops or essays") and on Sept 29 said one defendant's expulsion showed the panel did not weight the texts, which indicates her side knew the outcomes. The complaint itself says only, "upon information and belief," that the investigation has concluded. Federal rules require written notice of the outcome to both parties; whether and when Cornell sent it has not been reported.
  • Editorial, Sept 21 (opinion). "For the men to be back on campus, either the danger passed at some identifiable moment ... or it did not and the suspensions were lifted anyway." The Sun's own news story the same night could confirm only two of the seven as current students; per Kotlikoff, two were expelled, two suspended and one had graduated.
  • Letter to the editor, Sept 29, 4:30 pm (opinion). "There does not yet appear to be any reasonable explanation as to why only two of the undergraduates alleged to be gang-rapists received severe disciplinary action. Five others were allowed to write essays." Published 26 hours after Cornell's denial that essays were ever a "sole consequence," which the Sun had reported at 2:47 pm on Sept 28. The letter has been edited twice since, on Sept 29 (with a note correcting the signatories' titles) and on Oct 1 at 2:31 am (with no note); the sentence stands. Per Kotlikoff's breakdown, the "five others" were two suspended, one graduated and two found not responsible. Letter.
  • Guest column, Sept 27 (opinion). "only two of the seven perpetrators were expelled, leading one to wonder if those two submitted unsatisfactory essays." Two of the seven were found not responsible, per Kotlikoff and their own statements. Column.
  • News, Sept 18. "According to the complaint, Cornell's Title IX investigation is believed to have concluded, while the Ithaca Police Department is still investigating the incident." Ithaca Police said on Sept 28 that the incident "was not reported to the Ithaca Police Department." The Sun reported that in a separate story; the Sept 18 story carries two update notes (Sept 19, Sept 22) and no note on this point. Story.
  • News, Sept 28. The rugby story says three defendants "were accused of criminal sexual acts in the lawsuit." The lawsuit is civil; no criminal charge has been filed against anyone.
  • News, Sept 11, Nov 7 and Dec 5, 2025. Each dates the incident to "Oct. 25, 2024," following the wording of the Nov 2024 crime alert. The complaint, the DA and Cornell put it on Oct 19 to 20, 2024. The Sun's 2026 stories use Oct 19 without noting the change.
  • Corrections. No item labeled as a correction appears on any of the 47 pieces. Several carry update notes; none addresses the points above.

Patterns across outlets

  • "Seven men." Headlines across outlets, including the Sun's and many of this site's sources, describe a suit alleging that seven men gang-raped her. The complaint's allegations of intercourse are against four of the seven. The two defendants it accuses only of the ketamine conduct have been named in the same headlines, editorials and social posts as the four.
  • The first ten days. From Sept 18 to Sept 27 the public record consisted of the complaint, the plaintiff's attorney's statements, one defendant's lawyer's denial, and Cornell's Sept 21 statement. The DA's account of her 2024 statement, Cornell's denial on sanctions, the other defendants' statements, and the records from Cornell's own investigation all arrived on Sept 28 or later. Coverage and commentary from the first ten days, including the Sun's editorial and Rep. Ocasio-Cortez's Sept 27 town hall remarks, could not reflect any of it, and much of it has not been revisited.
  • The essays. The claim that the accused were let off with essays comes from one line of the complaint about interim measures in Nov 2024. Cornell denied on Sept 28 that essays were ever a "sole consequence" and confirmed expulsions and suspensions. The claim continued to circulate afterward: in the Sept 29 letter above, and in Rep. Ocasio-Cortez's Oct 1 remark, reported by the Sun, that "we make rapists write an essay in private." The record published Oct 1 shows where the kernel is: the one respondent found responsible only for the attempted offense, and not for the assault, was sanctioned with a reflection paper among other requirements. For the four found responsible for more, the sanctions were expulsion or suspension. No outlet that repeated the essays claim has reported that distinction.
  • What national outlets added. The material that complicates the complaint's account came almost entirely from outlets outside Ithaca: CBS New York (the texts, the transcript, the Title IX interviews), ABC News (the 2024 statement, the written decision, the hearing testimony, the full chat), The New York Times (the three weeks before she reported, the men's accounts), NBC News (the court file of one respondent's challenge), and CNN (the group chat photo, the Cornell Health visit). The same outlets also carried the strongest material for her account: "100% confidence I was raped," "90% ... not consensual," the rape-kit request, "I felt like lions around me." The Governor's Oct 2 press conference, and her order removing the DA, rested on that material.

Method: every item in the Sun's Chi Phi section and its archive from Nov 8, 2024 to Oct 2, 2026, 1:30 pm was read in full (47 items). "Not found" means at least two searches of the item's text turned up nothing. The audit will be updated as coverage changes, and corrected on notice.
