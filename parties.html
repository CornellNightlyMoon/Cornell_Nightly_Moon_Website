The parties and what they say

Who is involved and what each has said, in their own words, with sources.

Updated Oct 2, 2026, 2:30 pm Eastern

Confirmed primary document or acknowledged Alleged one side's claim, unproven Disputed the parties contradict Open unanswered How labels work

The plaintiff

"Jane Doe," a Cornell student at the time who was 20 years old on Oct 19, 2024, and is no longer a Cornell student. Represented by attorney Thomas Giuffra. She reported to CUPD on Nov 8, 2024, filed a formal Title IX complaint that Cornell took up in January 2025, and sued on Sept 14, 2026. The 20-year civil limitations period for conduct constituting a listed sex offense (CPLR 213-c) means the 2026 filing is timely for those claims.

The seven individual defendants

The complaint names seven then-members of the chapter as individual defendants. Their names are in the public filing and have been reported by the Cornell Daily Sun, CBS News and others. This page refers to them by the complaint's numbering (see the note on names).

What the complaint alleges against each. Alleged Members 1 and 2 initiated the first encounter and supplied the ketamine; member 2 sent the 1:42 am message. Members 2 and 4 had sex with her in a second encounter. Members 1 and 3 moved her to a smaller room for the final encounter. Member 5 kissed her and she pushed him away. All seven are alleged to have poured ketamine on her body. Members 6 and 7 are not alleged to have had any sexual contact with her.

What they have said publicly. Two of the seven (the complaint's members 6 and 7) have said, directly or through counsel, that they did not participate in any sexual activity or drug use, that they briefly entered the room, that they engaged "thoughtlessly" or made "an immature comment" on Snapchat, and that Cornell found them not responsible. Member 7's attorney says a hair-follicle test showed no ketamine, that "taking part in an offensive conversation is not the same as taking part in a rape," and that he has contacted the DA. Member 4, through counsel, "unequivocally denies the allegations" and says the claims "do not reflect the complete record." Members 1, 2, 3 and 5 have not publicly commented; member 1's lawyer declined to comment to The New York Times and member 2's did not respond. Confirmed as to what was said; the underlying claims are their own.

What they told Cornell's investigators is summarized under What the Title IX records add. All seven denied wrongdoing; most said she did not appear intoxicated.

Since Oct 1. Member 7's March 2026 court challenge to his finding, and its denial, became public through NBC News; his lawyer says he "is not accused of rape and never has been." Member 4's co-counsel told reporters the encounter was consensual and disputed the ketamine and group-chat allegations; a video of a student confronting him on campus was published by TMZ on Oct 2. No new statements from members 1, 2, 3, 5 or 6. Confirmed

Sources: CBS News, Sept 30; NBC Chicago, Sept 30; 13WHAM, Sept 30; ABC News, Sept 29; New York Times, Oct 1; NBC News, Oct 1; IBTimes, Oct 2

Cornell University

Named for negligence, negligent supervision, breach of contract, the New York State Human Rights Law and Education Law Article 129-B. Cornell's position, across its Sept 21 and 28 statements, its Sept 29 FAQ and its Sept 29 community email:

  • It investigated and adjudicated the allegations under university policy over several months, with a hearing panel of trained faculty and staff that heard evidence "over multiple days," and the panel "issued a range of sanctions, which included expulsions and suspensions." The records show more than 50 interviews, 12 days of hearings, 1,700 pages of hearing transcript, and a 99-page written decision. Confirmed
  • "Any suggestion that the university did not impose consequential punishments for those involved is false." A "continuation of the false narrative that there were minimal consequences in this case is irresponsible." (Sept 28.)
  • "Federal privacy law prohibits Cornell from disclosing specific information regarding individual students." (See FERPA for what the statute permits.)
  • The decision not to bring charges in 2024 "was made by the Tompkins County District Attorney, not Cornell." The FAQ first said CUPD "shared all investigatory information" with the DA; that sentence was deleted on Sept 30 and replaced on Oct 1 with: CUPD "shared the victim's sworn statement and the Snapchat screenshot." Cornell's media office: "The FAQ is a living document, and we are working to update it regularly." (FAQ; WSKG; Newsweek.)
  • CUPD officers are "special deputy sheriffs pursuant to NY Educational Law 5709"; "this incident occurred in Cornell's jurisdiction." (FAQ, as revised.)
  • On whether its 2024 response was adequate: it took the reports seriously and acted promptly, and "responding to an incident of this seriousness cannot end when an investigation is completed or sanctions are imposed. We have an obligation to ask what more we can learn, what we can do better." (FAQ.)
  • "The events that took place on our campus in 2024 were appalling then and remain so now." (Sept 29 email, signed by President Kotlikoff, Provost Bala and Board Chair Smalling.)
  • Oct 2, after the special prosecutor's appointment: "We fully support the appointment of a special prosecutor and efforts to change New York State's rape law and are committed to an independent external review of all aspects of how Cornell has handled this case." The outside counsel has not been named. President Kotlikoff did not attend the Oct 1 Student Assembly hearing. Confirmed

What Cornell has not said: whether the 120-page interview transcript was ever sent to the DA, and if not, why; who decided what to send; how many students were expelled, suspended or found not responsible (beyond the breakdown CBS attributes to Kotlikoff's Sept 29 remarks); whether it had prior reports about drug use at the house, including the ketamine overdose its own decision places one day before the incident; whether the complainant was notified of the outcome; anything about how the Title IX records reached the press; who the outside reviewer will be.

Tompkins County District Attorney Matthew Van Houten

Not a party to the lawsuit, and as of the evening of Oct 1 no longer the prosecutor: Executive Order 64 transferred the case to the Attorney General. His statement on the order: "I trust that she will examine the facts and apply the law. It has become clear that we were not provided the full picture by the University. The focus has been and should continue to be on the victims." His Sept 28 statement remains the most detailed public account of the 2024 decision. Its core:

"The facts provided by Jane Doe's statement do not allege that any person used physical force to compel Jane Doe to engage in sexual conduct." ... "do not allege that she was either rendered incapable of controlling her own conduct or that any drug was administered to her without her consent." ... "do not allege that she was rendered unconscious." ... "There is no evidence in Jane Doe's statement indicating that she clearly expressed that she did not consent." "According to the statement, each time Jane Doe asked to stop the sexual conduct, the sexual conduct stopped."

DA statement, Sept 28, 2026

He also wrote that the behavior of certain members "was without question disgusting and morally reprehensible," that "criminal prosecutions must be based upon evidence and not emotion," that his office relied on CUPD's investigation and did not independently investigate, and that a senior prosecutor with experience in campus sexual assault cases is preparing the grand jury presentation. He told CBS on Sept 30 that the transcript "changes the landscape for me" and that he would seek it from CUPD; he told CNN the same day, "At this time I need to shift my focus to the investigation and to pursuing the additional evidence that has recently come to light." On Oct 1 he said he had obtained the 120-page transcript and that it "confirms the contents of Jane Doe's sworn statement," called Giuffra's criticism "unwarranted and ill-informed," and said the case will go to a grand jury. Hours later the Governor removed the case from his office. Giuffra, on hearing of the appointment: "That's wonderful news ... my client and I had zero confidence in this district attorney's ability to prosecute this case." Confirmed

Chi Phi: the Xi chapter, the house association, and the national organization

The Chi Phi Chapter House Association (the Xi chapter's New York not-for-profit) is alleged to own and operate the house at 107 Edgemoor Lane; the complaint also alleges Cornell owned or controlled it. Chi Phi Fraternity, Inc. and the Chi Phi Educational Trust, both based in Georgia, are sued as "Chi Phi National." The former chapter president and unnamed faculty and alumni advisers and executive board members are also named. Alleged as to ownership and control.

Chi Phi's national organization said on Sept 18 that the allegations are "deeply concerning" and that it "unequivocally condemns sexual violence." On Sept 30, interim executive director Trey Robb said the Xi chapter "voted unanimously to remove the accused members from its rolls" three days after the Nov 2024 police report, that the Grand Council then expelled them permanently, that "these actions went beyond anything the law or Cornell University required of us," and that chapters "manage their own affairs" with headquarters relying on their reporting. He did not address the lawsuit's allegations directly, and repeated the account to Reuters on Oct 1. Cornell's fraternity and sorority life page lists the chapter's charter as "dormant." On Sept 30 the presidents of all 22 Interfraternity Council chapters voted unanimously to cancel the weekend's social events; the Panhellenic Council "unequivocally supports the decision." Confirmed

Delta Delta Delta (Tri Delta)

The Alpha Beta House Corporation and the national sorority are sued for negligence and breach of contract. The complaint alleges she drank to intoxication inside the sorority house before the event, that the chapter's house director saw her intoxicated and nevertheless took her to Moonies, and that the sorority failed to train its sober monitors. Alleged A Tri Delta representative told the Cornell Daily Sun the sorority denies the negligence allegations. No full statement has been published. Confirmed

Moonies Bar & Nightclub (Zareen Enterprise LLC)

Sued under New York's Dram Shop Act for allegedly serving her while under 21. Alleged Moonies says the claim is "completely inaccurate," that underage guests at the private sorority event were hand-marked and 21-plus guests wristbanded, and that she could not have been served while marked. Confirmed as to Moonies' position.

City of Ithaca

Not a party. Ithaca Police: "This incident was not reported to the Ithaca Police Department. The Ithaca Police Department learned of these allegations recently through the media." Mayor Robert Cantelmo: "Despite the severity of these crimes, Cornell University administrators and police failed to contact or coordinate any response with the Ithaca Police Department." The Common Council votes Oct 7 on his proposed moratorium on student event permits. Confirmed

State and federal officials

  • Gov. Kathy Hochul, Sept 29: "The allegations in this case are nothing short of horrifying." "I am calling for outside counsel to conduct a transparent, independent review of the university's response." Sept 30: Cornell has agreed. Oct 1: signs Executive Order 64 appointing the Attorney General special prosecutor under Executive Law § 63(2), saying newly released information had "undercut my faith, and the public's faith, in the District Attorney's ability to effectively investigate and prosecute the case at this time." Oct 2 press conference: "at every turn, she was failed by people and institutions with an obligation to protect her"; her words "never made it into the account that the Cornell police sent to prosecutors"; "In what world does the district attorney not even interview her, or anyone else involved, or even request the full transcript?"; "No institution gets to protect its reputation at the expense of a young person seeking justice"; "Voluntary intoxication is not a license for sexual assault or gang rape. Period." On Kotlikoff: "I don't have enough information to draw any further conclusions at this time." Confirmed
  • Attorney General Letitia James: now the special prosecutor, with authority to investigate, present to a grand jury and prosecute. Oct 1: "Every New Yorker deserves to know that when they report a crime, it will be investigated fully and fairly." Oct 2: "This is now an active criminal investigation led by the office of the attorney general." "We will review the facts, gather evidence, interview witnesses, and if warranted, pursue prosecutions." "At this point in time, we will not be focusing on the actions of the district attorney." "A comprehensive investigation of this nature takes time." Her office's separate review of Cornell's response, announced Sept 29, continues. Confirmed
  • Assembly Speaker Carl Heastie, Sept 30: a 14-member working group will examine the state's sexual assault laws, including provisions on voluntary intoxication, and deliver recommendations by year end for the 2027 session. Confirmed
  • Sen. Chuck Schumer, Sept 30: "There must now be a truly independent and exhaustive investigation, not only into what happened, but into how this case was handled." Confirmed
  • Sen. Kirsten Gillibrand is citing the case in support of the federal Campus Accountability and Safety Act; no hearing or vote is scheduled. Confirmed

Students and faculty

  • The Student Assembly held a public hearing on Oct 1 with about 100 attendees; no senior administrator was reported present. Its president: "We're absolutely ashamed of the events that happened on campus." Resolution 15 demands a town hall with Vice Presidents Lombardi and Kimball within 30 days, aggregate sanction data, the enrollment status of students found responsible, and an account of CUPD's coordination with the DA. Confirmed
  • The Interfraternity Council: all 22 chapter presidents voted to cancel the weekend's social events. Confirmed
  • Twenty student organizations have called a "Justice for Survivors" protest for Oct 5 on Ho Plaza, demanding expulsion of "all alleged assaulters," trauma-informed CUPD training, an external investigation, a return to an independent judicial system, and revisions to Policy 6.4 with minimum sanctions. Confirmed
  • The Cornell chapter of the AAUP, Oct 2: CUPD "not only egregiously mishandled, but omitted vital evidence to the Tompkins County District Attorney"; "In moments of crisis such as this, we need leaders, not administrators." Confirmed

Sources: New York Times, Oct 2; Resolution 15; Sun on the IFC; Sun on the protest; AAUP statement
