Disputed and open points

Where the parties contradict each other, and the questions no one has answered publicly.

Updated Oct 2, 2026, 2:30 pm Eastern

Did CUPD give the DA the Snapchat screenshot in Nov 2024? Cornell FAQ: "The Cornell University Police Department provided it as part of the evidence supplied to the District Attorney in November 2024." After two edits, the FAQ now says CUPD shared "the victim's sworn statement and the Snapchat screenshot" with the DA. The DA to ABC, Sept 29: his office "never received" the image. To CNN, Sept 30: he got "the six page statement and nothing more except for the Snapchat video"; the screenshots "were not part of our initial analysis, but I understand that Cornell had them." Disputed
Did the DA receive the Nov 14 to 15 interview transcript in 2024? The DA, Sept 30: "I never saw it, never heard it until tonight." "We never received it and still don't have it. It was not part of what Cornell provided." Oct 1: he has "now obtained" the 120-page transcript, which was "produced from a recording by a Title IX investigator who was in the room," and it "confirms the contents of Jane Doe's sworn statement." Cornell's FAQ originally said CUPD "shared all investigatory information" with the DA. That sentence was deleted on Sept 30 and replaced on Oct 1 with: CUPD "shared the victim's sworn statement and the Snapchat screenshot." The transcript is not mentioned. Gov. Hochul, Oct 2: her words "never made it into the account that the Cornell police sent to prosecutors"; sending "a partial record over to the district attorney, in my judgment, is a failure." The DA, Oct 1: "we were not provided the full picture by the University." Cornell has not addressed the transcript directly. Confirmed by Cornell's revised wording and the Governor that the transcript was not sent; Open why
Does the transcript change the case? The DA, Sept 30, before reading it: "that changes the landscape for me." Oct 1, after obtaining it: it "confirms the contents of Jane Doe's sworn statement." Gov. Hochul's executive order: the sworn statement "reportedly omitted key portions of Jane Doe's account, including Jane Doe's unequivocal assertion that she had been sexually assaulted." Giuffra: that the DA's office "did not even review the transcripts of the police interviews before deciding to not prosecute is shocking to me." The AP, from the transcript: "I can say with 100% confidence I was raped," "completely and totally incapacitated," "physically hit very, very hard." Disputed The question now belongs to the Attorney General.
Were the accused allowed to "mitigate their conduct by submitting essays"? Complaint ¶181: the seven "were afforded the opportunity to mitigate their conduct by submitting essays to CORNELL" after the Nov 2024 interim suspensions. Giuffra: five "faced lesser sanctions, including suspensions, workshops or essays." Cornell, Sept 28: "None of the individuals charged were offered the opportunity to write essays as a sole consequence of their involvement." FAQ: asked whether students "only had to write essays," the answer is "No." Court records show that the one respondent found responsible only for attempted sexual exploitation was sanctioned with a reflection paper, an educational proposal, a no-contact order, participation in a study, and programming work. Disputed in framing. A written reflection was part of one respondent's final sanction; it was not his sole consequence, and the others' sanctions, per Kotlikoff, were expulsion or suspension.
What were the sanctions? Cornell: "a range of sanctions, which included expulsions and suspensions"; declines further detail, citing FERPA. Giuffra: two expelled, which his client "does not consider justice." CBS News, Sept 30, attributing to President Kotlikoff at a Sept 29 Student Assembly meeting: two expelled, two suspended for at least two semesters, one had already graduated, two found not responsible for sexual misconduct. Two of the seven say Cornell found them not responsible. Court records: member 7 was found responsible for "attempted sexual exploitation" and not for the assault, and sanctioned as described above; how that fits the "two not responsible" count has not been explained. No official per-student breakdown exists. Confirmed in outline; Open in detail
Was a rape kit collected? The Title IX records, per The New York Times and CNN: she visited Cornell Health on Oct 22, 2024 for a UTI and STI testing, said the infection came "in a traumatic way," and was advised to speak with someone; no kit was offered. She requested a rape kit at an Ithaca emergency room on Nov 7. Giuffra: "Timing is vital for rape kits because DNA evidence is lost over time." Cornell has not addressed the Oct 22 visit. Whether a kit was collected on Nov 7, and with what result, has not been reported. Open
Was she served alcohol at Moonies? Complaint: served at least one drink despite under-21 hand marks. Moonies, Sept 28: "completely inaccurate"; about 185 guests and about 35 underage at a private sorority event; underage guests were hand-marked; asks how she entered another bar afterward while marked. Disputed
Was the Ithaca Police Department investigating? Complaint ¶187: the matter was "still being investigated by the Ithaca Police Department." IPD, Sept 28: "This incident was not reported to the Ithaca Police Department"; it learned of the allegations "through the media." Cornell: CUPD officers are sworn Tompkins County deputy sheriffs and "this incident occurred in Cornell's jurisdiction." Confirmed IPD's statement is unrebutted
Was the drug use voluntary? The DA: her statement "described her participation in drug use ... as voluntary"; "She never says that she was drugged without her knowledge." Complaint: she was "pressured" and "commanded." AP, from the transcript: she said she was pressured into ketamine, marijuana and alcohol. Disputed Legally decisive; see Consent
Did the DA's office try to meet with her? The DA: he asked CUPD to tell her "that we would personally meet with her"; "neither Jane Doe nor her attorneys ever contacted the DA's Office" in two years. Giuffra: she "never spoke with anyone other than the officer who took her original complaint" and was never contacted by investigators "trained in interviewing rape survivors." Confirmed No meeting happened; whose responsibility that was is Disputed
Did Cornell have prior warning about drug use at the house? Fingerlakes1 reports the complaint alleges a "ketamine culture" at the house and a drug-related police response there about a week before Oct 19, 2024. Cornell has not addressed this publicly. Alleged Open
Was she told the outcome of Cornell's process? The complaint (¶186) says only that "upon information and belief" Cornell's investigation "is concluded." The Sun's editorial says she was not told the results. Cornell has not addressed this. The Clery Act requires simultaneous written notice of the outcome to both parties. Open
