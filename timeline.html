  1. About Oct 18, 2024

    Cornell's 99-page written decision in the case, as quoted by ABC News: "Ketamine was a substance used by multiple individuals in the Chi Phi house at the time, including [redacted] who had overdosed on or about October 18, 2024." Confirmed as the report's finding

    ABC News, Sept 30, 2026

  2. Oct 19 to 20, 2024

    The night in question. Per the complaint: she drinks at the sorority house, leaves about 8:30 pm for a sorority event at Moonies Bar, moves to Lot 10 about 10 pm, and walks alone to the Chi Phi house about 11 pm to see a member she knew. At about 1:42 am a member posts to the chapter's "Chi Phi Actives" Snapchat group that there is "free p*ssy" upstairs. Per the complaint the assault ends about 5:45 am. Alleged The group chat exists and has now been published nearly in full: after the 1:42 am message, a photo of her in the room, in which she "appears to be smiling with another man" (CBS); "Like you can walk in and whip it out rn"; "Boys come whip it out," sent per ABC by the member who invited her; "laying rn"; "Shop still open?" and "Yeah"; and at about 2:30 am "Are you guys at least naked?", to which member 7 replied "not a bit." Confirmed as the chat's contents

    Complaint ¶¶135 to 174; CBS New York, Sept 29; CBS News, Oct 2; ABC News, Oct 2; NBC News, Oct 1

  3. Oct 20, 2024

    About 11:15 am she returns to the sorority house, still in the previous night's clothes, and tells roommates she took ketamine and had sexual contact with multiple men. Per the roommates' Title IX interviews she did not use the words "rape" or "assault"; one said she was "in shock," "not really understanding the gravity of what she was saying"; another said she said "Oh my goodness, isn't that crazy?" while laughing, and separately "I wasn't comfortable." Confirmed as the roommates' accounts

    New York Times, Oct 1, 2026; CBS New York, Oct 1, 2026

  4. Oct 21, 2024

    She tells her sorority "big sister" rumors are circulating that she is a "slut," adding, with a laugh, "unfortunately they are true"; the big sister told investigators she "had not yet processed what had happened." The same day member 2 texts her: "I just wanted to apologize for how things went down the other night," saying he and member 1 were too intoxicated to "shut it down and kick people out before it got way out of hand." She replies: "At the end of the day non of the sexual stuff was illegal and honestly i really liked being w u and [member 1]," and that any future contact should leave out "the extra 5 billion men and ketamine." In the same exchange, per ABC: "All i remember after that was you giving me ket[amine]." Member 1 texts her separately about "how fast nonsense spreads at this school" and that she "did not have anything to be ashamed of." Confirmed as the texts' contents

    New York Times, Oct 1; CBS New York, Sept 29, 2026; ABC News, Oct 2

  5. Oct 22, 2024

    She visits Cornell Health for a urinary tract infection and STI testing and tells the provider she contracted it "in a traumatic way." Per the records the provider suggested she speak with someone; no rape kit was offered or collected. Confirmed as the records describe it

    New York Times, Oct 1; CNN, Oct 1, 2026

  6. Oct 23 to 28, 2024

    Oct 23: she texts member 1, "Bro, everyone knows," that the episode is "RUINING my life temporarily," and "my reputation is destroyed and I'm utterly ashamed"; she tells a roommate that if anyone asks, "say it didn't happen." Oct 25: member 1 texts "U good?"; she replies that member 2 "def told people" and later sends him an online forum post accusing Chi Phi of gang rape. Oct 28: on a phone call, overheard by a roommate, she asks member 1, "How could you let this happen?" Around Halloween he invites her to a chapter party ("chi phi is your house and you are welcome here"; she replies "thank u i really appreciate that"); the chapter president has her disinvited; she texts, "I knew this was going to happen. The woman will always get the short end of the stick. Yall aren't my friends. Yall are covering your asses." Also from the records: "I'm f*cking pissed cause this many people shouldn't know." Confirmed as the records describe it

    New York Times, Oct 1; ABC News, Sept 30; NBC News, Oct 1

  7. Nov 3, 2024

    She texts member 2: "let's be clear that 90% of what happened that night was not consensual. i was given more key than i can remember," and that she had performed a sex act "in front of everyone like a sex doll/worker." Confirmed as the text's contents

    New York Times, Oct 1; CNN, Oct 1

  8. Nov 7, 2024

    She goes to an Ithaca emergency room and requests a rape kit, 19 days after the incident. The same day she texts member 1: "I haven't said anything but I do know people are changing the narrative because like in any sexual scenario w one woman and many men the woman was probably the victim," and "i know there are rumors i was gang banged." A member texts her: "I understand the regret, but personally I don't think you have anything to be ashamed of"; "The only shameful thing is how this spread and how people won't shut up about it." Confirmed

    New York Times, Oct 1; ABC News, Sept 30; NBC News, Oct 1

  9. Nov 8, 2024

    She reports to CUPD. She texts member 1: "I think I'm done talking to you. I know you lied." The same day Cornell issues a temporary suspension of the Chi Phi Xi chapter and interim suspensions of the accused students. Confirmed

    Cornell statement, Nov 26, 2024; complaint ¶¶179 to 181; ABC News, Sept 30

  10. About Nov 11, 2024

    Chi Phi's national organization says the Xi chapter voted unanimously to remove the accused members from its rolls "three days after" the police report, and that its Grand Council then expelled them permanently. Confirmed (Chi Phi's own account; not independently verified.)

    CBS New York, Sept 30, 2026

  11. Nov 14 to 15, 2024

    CUPD interviews her over two days, with a Title IX investigator, a victim advocate and an adviser present; the recording runs to a 120-page transcript. On Nov 15 she signs a six-page typed statement with more than twenty handwritten notations. The statement includes both "pretty ok with having this threesome" (the DA's quotation) and "I don't remember much of this threesome. I was pretty incapacitated," "I was instantly horrified," "I was starting to become completely numb to the whole situation," and "my phone was across the room and I felt like I couldn't get to it, as well as I didn't have any form of payment to order an Uber" (NBC's quotations). Confirmed

    AP via ABC News, Sept 30, 2026; DA statement, Sept 28, 2026

  12. Mid-Nov 2024

    The Chi Phi house is vandalized by an activist group after the suspension becomes public. Confirmed

    Fox News, Nov 15, 2024

  13. Nov 2024

    CUPD asks the DA's office to review her written statement "to determine if her allegations constituted a crime under New York law." The DA concludes the facts in the statement "objectively prevent the filing of criminal charges." The DA says he asked CUPD to tell her his office would meet with her personally; both sides agree no meeting ever happened. Confirmed

    DA statement, Sept 28, 2026

  14. Nov 26, 2024

    Interim President Michael Kotlikoff issues a statement on "serious and deeply disturbing allegations of drug abuse and sexual violence at the Chi Phi fraternity house," confirming the chapter's suspension and interim student suspensions. Confirmed

    Cornell statement

  15. Jan 9, 2025

    A federal court vacates the 2024 Title IX regulations nationwide (Tennessee v. Cardona); the 2020 regulations, which require a live hearing with cross-examination at colleges, apply again. Confirmed (Context for how Cornell's hearing was run.)

  16. Jan 14, 2025

    Cornell opens a formal Title IX investigation on her formal complaint. Transcripts of the investigative interviews are distributed to the parties the same month. Over the investigation a Cornell investigator conducts more than 50 interviews (32 witnesses, the seven respondents and others); the May 2025 hearings run 12 days and more than 1,700 transcript pages; the panel's written decision is 99 pages; the full record is 4,000 to 5,000 pages, per court records in one defendant's later challenge. Confirmed

    Complaint ¶183; TIME, Sept 30, 2026; CBS New York, Oct 1; CNY Central, Oct 2, from court records

  17. Feb 6, 2025

    Cornell announces the Presidential Task Force on Campus Sexual Assault. Confirmed

    Cornell statement

  18. May 2 to 29, 2025

    A hearing panel of trained faculty and staff hears the case. The complaint says there were "at least twelve" hearings; The New York Times, from the records, says "at least a dozen"; Cornell says the panel "heard evidence over multiple days." She testifies: "I felt like bait. I felt like lions around me. It just was a totally debilitating place to be in"; on ketamine: "You look like an operating human being ... you don't look dead or asleep necessarily. So lights are on, but nobody's home." From her Title IX interviews: "I was like literally cornered"; "I'm a naked woman and they are getting in bed with me." Confirmed

    Complaint ¶184; Cornell statement, Sept 28, 2026; ABC News, Sept 30; New York Times, Oct 1; NBC News, Oct 1; CBS News, Oct 1

  19. 2025, date not public

    The panel imposes sanctions. Cornell: "a range of sanctions, which included expulsions and suspensions." Per CBS, citing Kotlikoff: two expelled, two suspended at least two semesters, one had graduated, two found not responsible for sexual misconduct. Court records show member 7 was found responsible for "attempted sexual exploitation" for entering the room after the group-chat messages and for his reply in the chat, and not for the assault; his sanctions were a reflection paper, a proposal for educational materials on social media and sexual harassment, a no-contact order, participation in an Office of Civil Rights study, and help creating programming. The complaint says only that "upon information and belief" the investigation has concluded. Confirmed in outline; details under Disputed and open points.

    CBS News, Sept 30; NBC News, Oct 1

  20. Nov 4, 2025

    Cornell releases its 2025 Survey on Sexual Assault and Related Misconduct, showing increases across measures. Confirmed

    Complaint ¶185; Task Force final report

  21. March 2026

    Member 7 files an Article 78 proceeding in Tompkins County Supreme Court asking the court to annul Cornell's finding that he was responsible for attempted sexual exploitation. Justice Mark Masler later denies it, ruling the finding "cannot be considered to have been arbitrary and capricious." The hearing transcript and investigation records filed in that case are the source of much of what NBC News and CNY Central reported on Oct 1 and 2. Confirmed

    NBC News, Oct 1, 2026; CNY Central, Oct 2

  22. March 17, 2026

    The Task Force issues its final recommendations. Confirmed

    Cornell statement

  23. Sept 14, 2026

    The complaint is filed in the Supreme Court of the State of New York, New York County, Index No. 161704/2026. (CNN reports the filing as Sept 16.) Confirmed

    Complaint on NYSCEF

  24. Sept 18, 2026

    The DA says this is the day he learned of the complaint. The Cornell Daily Sun publishes the first report of the lawsuit. Chi Phi's national organization issues a first statement calling the allegations "deeply concerning." Confirmed

    Cornell Daily Sun; DA statement

  25. Sept 21, 2026

    Cornell's first statement on the lawsuit (Kyle Kimball, VP for University Relations): the matter was "investigated and adjudicated" under university policy; the chapter "remains barred"; federal privacy law bars disclosing individual outcomes. The Sun publishes the editorial "Cornell Won't, We Will." Confirmed

    Cornell statement; Sun editorial

  26. Sept 28, 2026

    The DA publishes a nine-page statement explaining the 2024 decision and announcing the case will go to a grand jury. Cornell issues a statement supporting the grand jury and saying no student was offered essays "as a sole consequence." The Ithaca Police Department says the incident "was not reported to the Ithaca Police Department" and it learned of it "through the media." Ithaca's mayor criticizes Cornell for not coordinating with city police. Moonies denies serving her. Confirmed

    DA statement; Cornell statement; Sun on IPD; TMZ on Moonies

  27. Sept 29, 2026

    Cornell publishes an 18-question FAQ and a community email signed by the president, provost and board chair. Gov. Hochul calls the allegations "nothing short of horrifying" and asks for an independent outside-counsel review of Cornell's response. The Attorney General's office confirms it is reviewing Cornell's response. CBS New York reports the Snapchat chain and, separately, the Oct 21, 2024 texts between her and one of the men. President Kotlikoff, at a Student Assembly meeting, gives the only numeric breakdown of sanctions reported so far (see Disputed and open points). Confirmed

    Cornell FAQ; Cornell email; WSKG on Hochul; CNN on the AG; CBS New York on the texts

  28. Sept 30, 2026

    The AP and CBS New York publish her Nov 2024 interview transcript; the DA says he never received it and that it "changes the landscape for me." ABC News publishes excerpts of Cornell's 99-page written decision in the case and her 2025 hearing testimony. Cornell updates its FAQ to say CUPD gave the DA the Snapchat screenshot in Nov 2024; the DA says it was not part of his analysis. Chi Phi's interim executive director says the chapter removed the accused within days in 2024. Sen. Schumer calls for "a truly independent and exhaustive investigation." Assembly Speaker Heastie forms a 14-member working group on the state's sexual assault laws. Gov. Hochul says in a recorded video that she spoke with President Kotlikoff and Cornell has agreed to the independent review, with an announcement to come "in a fairly short order." Confirmed

    AP; CBS New York; ABC News; Sun; CBS6 Albany; Sun on Hochul

  29. Oct 1, 2026, daytime

    The New York Times publishes two reports from "hundreds of pages" of Cornell's Title IX files: a day-by-day account of the three weeks before she reported, and the accused men's own accounts to investigators. CBS New York publishes the Title IX interview transcripts. The DA says in a written statement that he has now obtained the 120-page transcript of her police interview, that it "confirms the contents of Jane Doe's sworn statement," and that he will still present the case to a grand jury. Attorney General James, on X, calls the allegations "horrific and unacceptable." Rep. Nancy Pelosi cancels an Oct 15 Cornell event. Cornell's FAQ is edited: the sentence saying CUPD shared "all investigatory information" with the DA is removed (WSKG reported the deletion on Sept 30 afternoon) and later replaced with one saying CUPD shared "the victim's sworn statement and the Snapchat screenshot." Confirmed

    NYT, "How a Cornell student went from shame to seeking justice"; NYT, "Men accused ... give varying accounts"; CBS New York; CNY Central on the DA; Sun on the AG; WSKG on the FAQ edit; Sun on the FAQ edit

  30. Oct 1, 2026, 4:45 pm

    The Student Assembly's public hearing at Willard Straight Hall draws about 100 people; President Kotlikoff does not attend and "few school leaders were in the room" (NYT). SA President Eeshaan Chaudhuri opens: "We're absolutely ashamed of the events that happened on campus." Students describe their own Title IX experiences and a campus "mental health crisis." Resolution 15, demanding a town hall with senior administrators within 30 days, aggregate sanction data, the enrollment status of students found responsible, and an account of CUPD's coordination with the DA, is on the agenda; the Assembly's site does not yet record a vote. Confirmed

    New York Times, Oct 2; CBS New York, Oct 2; Resolution 15

  31. Oct 1, 2026, evening

    Gov. Hochul signs Executive Order 64 appointing Attorney General Letitia James special prosecutor, superseding the Tompkins County DA. Her statement: newly released information "continues to raise serious questions about the investigation conducted by the Cornell Police Department and the decision by the Tompkins County District Attorney not to prosecute," which has "undercut my faith, and the public's faith, in the District Attorney's ability to effectively investigate and prosecute the case." The order recites that the DA declined "without speaking to Jane Doe," relying on a sworn statement that "reportedly omitted key portions of Jane Doe's account, including Jane Doe's unequivocal assertion that she had been sexually assaulted." The DA accepts: "It has become clear that we were not provided the full picture by the University." James: "we do not take that responsibility lightly." The same evening NBC News publishes records from member 7's court challenge, including his sanctions and the group chat's "not a bit" reply; CNN publishes the photo of her in the house; CBS News publishes a compilation with new quotes from her police interview and Title IX interviews. Her attorney, told of the appointment on air: "That's wonderful news." Confirmed

    Executive Order 64; Hochul statement; James statement; Fox 56 on the DA's statement; NBC News; CBS News; NewsNation

  32. Oct 2, 2026

    Hochul and James hold a press conference in Manhattan. Hochul: "She sat for hours and told them exactly what happened to her. And according to new reports, she could not have been clearer that she had been 100% confident that she had been raped. Yet, shockingly, those words never made it into the account that the Cornell police sent to prosecutors." "For them to send a partial record over to the district attorney, in my judgment, is a failure." "In what world does the district attorney not even interview her, or anyone else involved, or even request the full transcript?" "Voluntary intoxication is not a license for sexual assault or gang rape. Period." On President Kotlikoff: "I don't have enough information to draw any further conclusions at this time." James: "This is now an active criminal investigation led by the office of the attorney general." "We will review the facts, gather evidence, interview witnesses, and if warranted, pursue prosecutions." "At this point in time, we will not be focusing on the actions of the district attorney." Cornell: "We fully support the appointment of a special prosecutor and efforts to change New York State's rape law and are committed to an independent external review of all aspects of how Cornell has handled this case." ABC publishes the full group chat and photographs from the investigation file; CBS publishes the photo of her in the room, obtained through her attorney. All 22 IFC chapters cancel the weekend's social events. The Cornell AAUP chapter says CUPD "omitted vital evidence" and calls for "leaders, not administrators." Twenty student groups announce an Oct 5 protest. Confirmed

    Press conference video, C-SPAN; amNY; CBS New York; UPI; ABC News; CBS News on the photo; Sun on the IFC; AAUP statement; Sun on the protest

  33. Oct 5 and 7, 2026

    Scheduled: Oct 5, 4:30 pm, "Justice for Survivors" protest on Ho Plaza. Oct 7: Ithaca Common Council vote on the mayor's proposed moratorium on student event permits.

  34. Oct 19 to 20, 2026

    Two years since the incident. Under CPL 30.10(2)(c), the limitations period for misdemeanor offenses is two years; felony periods are longer. See Statutes of limitation.
